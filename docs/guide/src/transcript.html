1 – Title 15 s

Shivaansh

Good morning ma'am. I'm Shivaansh, and this is Devansh. Our scheduler predicts how long a program will run next and uses that to decide who runs and for how long. In this review we also asked what happens when the prediction is wrong.

2 – The problem and our approach 40 s

Shivaansh

SJF is optimal but needs the future, and Round Robin's quantum is fixed by hand. Predicting bursts fixes that, but a predicting scheduler trusts its prediction: a long burst predicted as short keeps looking short every time it comes back from a time slice.

Devansh

So we use the prediction twice: to pick who runs, with ageing, and to set how long it runs from the 80% rule. And we added an overrun guard: once a burst clearly outruns its prediction, its estimate grows with the CPU time it has used.

3 – How we evaluated it 25 s

Shivaansh

7 workloads, 14 configurations, 10 evaluation seeds, all paired. Training, tuning and evaluation used separate seeds, every claim is checked with paired tests and a Holm correction, and 53 tests pass. W7 is deliberately adversarial: bait processes use 76% of the CPU.

4 – Main result 25 s

Shivaansh

On the mixed workload we wait 27% less than SJF, better on all 10 seeds and significant. We close 54% of the gap to the oracle and use 61% fewer switches than Round Robin with 5 ms.

5 – Across the six standard workloads 30 s

Devansh

The clear win is the mixed workload, and the worst case drops by about half on CPU-bound and phase-changing work. The quantum adapts to each workload, and prediction alone gives only 5%. Honestly, on all-I/O work we are 14% worse, and that loss is significant.

6 – How much prediction quality matters 30 s

Devansh

We fed our scheduler and plain SJF predictions with controlled error. With perfect predictions our mechanism still beats SJF by 28%, so the mechanism matters most. Perfect predictions would give another 22%, and real errors hurt more than random ones.

7 – The weakness 30 s

Shivaansh

A bait process runs tiny bursts, so the model predicts 1.2 ms, then it runs for hundreds. Without a guard it keeps looking like 1.2 ms and is picked again. The guard sets its time left to a quarter of the CPU time used, never less than the prediction: after 40 ms it looks like 10 ms.

8 – Live demo on W7 35 s

Devansh

Optional: run python demo_innovation.py in Terminal (about 10 s); otherwise point at the screenshot.

Without the guard we lose to Round Robin and MLFQ. With it, over ten seeds, the average wait drops 21.5% and interactive waiting 92%. But MLFQ still has a 9% lower average on W7, and our worst case is about twice theirs.

9 – Trade-offs and a negative result 30 s

Shivaansh

On the five standard workloads the guard changes the average by less than 2%, none significant. We also made the ageing credit persistent; it raised waits by 17% under overload and 51% on W7, so we did not keep it.

10 – Comparison with existing schedulers 20 s

Shivaansh

On the mixed workload we beat every classical scheduler except SRTF, which is 3% better but not significantly, with a 35% higher worst case. On W7, MLFQ has the lowest average; with the guard we beat Round Robin.

11 – Conclusion and future work 20 s

Shivaansh

Using the prediction twice gives 27% less waiting, mainly thanks to the mechanism. The guard fixes a real weakness of prediction-based scheduling, but it is not a complete defence. Next: real traces and xv6. Thank you, ma'am.

If asked – one-line answers

Why ML? (Devansh) 12–22% more accurate than exponential averaging, but the sweep shows the mechanism matters more.

What if the prediction is wrong? (Devansh) That is what W7 and the guard test: interactive waiting −92%, but MLFQ still wins on average there.

Is W7 fair? (Shivaansh) It is deliberately adversarial, not typical; we say so, and the guard was tuned on seeds 100–102 and tested on 0–9.

Is it significant? (Shivaansh) Paired tests on 10 seeds with Holm correction; we only call a result significant if p < 0.05 after correction.

Real OS? (Shivaansh) It's a controlled simulation; a kernel would need a smaller model, and xv6 is next.
